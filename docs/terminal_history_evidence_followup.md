# Terminal-history evidence follow-up

Date: 2026-09-08. Scope: bounded empirical follow-up alongside the fresh
stochastic screen; no changes to model assumptions, observation inputs or
removal forcing.

## Disposition

**The new material does not identify detection probabilities, comparable survey
exposure or a complete natural-range removal schedule.** It strengthens the
historical interpretation of transfers and provides another explicitly dated,
rough population estimate. The current screening experiment should retain its
declared assumptions. A later removal analysis must distinguish wild collection,
captive holding, mortality and release into the insurance population.

The established findings and observation conventions remain in
[the observation-repair pilot](observation_repair_pilot.md), with the earlier
[evidence trace](../data/historical_fit_observation_audit/evidence_trace.csv).
This note records the additional search and its limits rather than replacing
those records.

## Newly verified agency material

The official [IFS newsletter archive](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/publications/previous-newsletters)
provides scanned issues from the relevant period. The key new source is
*On the Rise*, volume 26, number 1, May 1997, printed/PDF page 3,
“Promising news on most-endangered fish”. Its page image was visually checked
against local OCR. It reports:

- An estimate of **50–200 fish in 1992**, without its derivation or an explicit
  definition of which wild, captive or translocated fish were included.
- **18 fish moved to Oberon in late 1991 and another 13 in 1992**. The article
  associates the first group with fish remaining from the captive program.
- Additional Lake Pedder surveys were then being undertaken to obtain broodstock
  for Strathgordon. No visit dates, search effort or catches are supplied.
- Oberon breeding evidence was obtained in February 1997, followed by video
  evidence announced in March. These are insurance-population observations,
  not natural-range detections.

[Source: IFC newsletter, May 1997, p. 3](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/media/publications/IFS_Newsletter_VOL26-_NO1-_MAY1997.pdf#page=3).

The transfer split corroborates 31 releases across 1991–1992, but does not date
wild removals. The population estimate remains contextual; its different date
and undefined scope prevent a direct contradiction test against the later
retrospective fewer-than-50 statement.

An October 1996 agency article also describes continuing efforts to find further
Pedder populations while preventing redfin invasion. It provides no quantitative
survey information and is not an additional nondetection episode.
[Source: IFC newsletter, volume 25, number 2, October 1996, p. 7,
“Lakes Gordon and Pedder”](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/media/publications/IFS_Newsletter_VOL25-_NO2-_OCT1996.pdf#page=7).

| Wild removal schedule | No complete schedule recovered. The known January 1996 collection and May 1997 insurance transfer must retain different dates; the new release summary supplies no missing collection dates. |
| Population-estimate derivation | The newly found 1992 estimate is also unsupported by an estimation method. The scope and derivation of the circa-1995 fewer-than-50 estimate remain unknown. |
| Nondetection independence | No evidence identifies independent annual detection trials or quantifies shared effects of locations, observers, methods, weather or accessibility. Repeated publications are not repeat surveys. |
| IFS field logbooks, especially 1992–2002 | No directly accessible logs found; the earlier official assessment's unpublished-logbook reference remains the concrete retrieval lead. |

For future design, the empirical gaps favour preserving sensitivity scenarios
rather than fitting unidentified exposure parameters. Collection for conservation
can change the population being observed; a later demographic reconstruction
should account for that connection once the dates and numbers can be defended.
This follow-up supplies no basis for changing the current screen's detection
values, independence assumption, annual timing proxy or demographic forcing.

## Bounded search record

Two routes were used: targeted catalogue/archive discovery and direct inspection
of the agency publication archive. Searches focused on the Hamr report and
1990s IFC annual reports through Libraries Tasmania, the National Library
catalogue, STORS and the agency domain. No original Hamr PDF or field-logbook
download was located. This is a bounded retrieval result, not a claim that the
records do not exist or cannot be obtained.

The live [annual-report index](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/publications/annual-reports)
exposed reports for 2012–2013 onwards; the
[native-fish report index](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/publications/native-fish-reports)
exposed recent reports. Neither index supplied the requested 1990s report/logbook
material. Directly linked newsletter scans were therefore inspected instead.

All pages of these nine issues were screened with OCR; relevant passages were
read, and the May 1997 page carrying the quantitative findings was visually
verified:

| Issue | Result relevant to this task |
| --- | --- |
| Volume 20 no. 2, October 1991 | No new Pedder capture, removal or exposure record located. |
| Volume 21 no. 1, March 1992 | No new record located. |
| Volume 21 no. 2, October 1992 | No new record located. |
| Volume 22 no. 1, April 1993 | No new record located. |
| Volume 23 no. 1, May 1994 | No new record located. |
| Volume 24 no. 1, February 1995 | No new record located. |
| Volume 25 no. 1, June 1996 | No new record located. |
| Volume 25 no. 2, October 1996 | Continuing-search narrative, p. 7; no measured effort or dated outcome. |
| Volume 26 no. 1, May 1997 | Population-estimate year/range, annual release split and continuing-search narrative, p. 3. |

The archive links identify each issue. The scans have no usable embedded text
layer; OCR screening can miss content and these negative results are not an
exhaustive archive audit. Unchecked issues remain possible leads. After the
additional early-1990s issues yielded no further material evidence, inspection
stopped rather than expanding into an unrestricted historical search. No
correspondence was sent. Downloaded scans and renderings were temporary working
material; this note is the only repository file produced by the follow-up.
