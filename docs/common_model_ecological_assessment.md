# Ecological assessment of the retained historical alternatives

20 September 2026. Barry authorised assessment of the frozen examples against
historical population and survey evidence before re-contacting Fable. This is
an exploratory scientific assessment, not a revised calibration, new test on
held-out evidence, or change to the signed protocol. No new histories were
generated and no observation probabilities, cutoffs or compatibility labels
were changed.

## Assessment

**The alternatives face different ecological objections, and the current
evidence cannot supply a defensible probability ranking.** Modest abundance
is generally easier to conceal than sustained high abundance, conditional on
model counts representing surveyed fish. However, some low-abundance histories
rebound from one fish to dozens through a kernel with no mate limitation. Their
demographic interpretation is therefore also problematic. Extinction after the
final captures and low-abundance persistence both remain candidate explanations
of the late record; neither class is automatically validated. No restriction
is newly excluded by this assessment.

Some retained high-abundance examples require a substantial explanation for
continued nondetection. Their admission to a permissive compatibility set does
not establish that they make the historical observations unsurprising. Low q,
uneven coverage and uncertain population scale are materially different
explanations for that admission; the current analysis cannot identify which
applies. Equifinality here combines demographic alternatives with unresolved
observation and scale assumptions.

The assessment also limits the interpretation of the lake compartment. The
kernel pools reproduction across lake and refuge adults before allocating
recruits between compartments. A persistent lake count therefore does not
demonstrate an independently breeding lake population. Calling it a second
ecological refuge would go beyond the model output.

## Evidence that bears on the alternatives

The following evidence is already in the project record. Repeated summaries
of the same survey programme do not count as independent observations.

| Evidence and source | What it supports | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Hamr's 1988–91 survey record and Sanger's synthesis: contraction to tributaries; 68 captures in 43 Bonnet Bay visits; 117 surveyed locations and a cumulative five-stream detection footprint | A severe geographical contraction and repeatedly searched final refuge streams | 68 distinct fish, equal exposure per visit, five simultaneous populations, or a numerical detection bound |
| Hamr 1995: final populations in two Bonnet Bay streams, concentrated in covered, slow-flowing lower reaches, extending up to about 2 km upstream | Specific habitat and locations in which a remnant population could persist | Habitat area, carrying capacity, or proof that other suitable patches were absent |

| 2023 listing statement: retrospective estimate below 50 around the 1995 listing | Additional context for the small-population interpretation | A new independent census or an identified natural-range abundance likelihood |

## What the selected sources require

The previous selection is retained: twelve base profiles (minimum, middle and
maximum weighted survival per restriction at q=.02/shared quality), the four
across-configuration minima and both high-q boundary profiles. Duplicate roles
give 17 distinct profiles. Several survival ranks tie, so the labels are
source-ID-tied illustrations, not a gradient or a representative sample.

For the base examples, this table makes the population burden more concrete:

| Restriction | Factual-weighted refuge mean in actual 1995, min/middle/max selected roles | Mean total in 1997–2001 given survival through 2002, same roles |
|---|---:|---:|
| R_H | 22 / 133 / 157 | 30 / 170 / 175 |
| R_T | 151 / 93 / 202 | 182 / 114 / 218 |
| R_C | 31 / 180 / 201 | 23 / 182 / 206 |
| R_J | 41 / 96 / 139 | 37 / 104 / 149 |

The columns are different estimands: the first averages all histories with
factual weights, whereas the second conditions additionally on terminal
survival. Neither is an observed population estimate. All twelve base profiles
meet the survivor-weight concentration criteria. The underlying values and
the five boundary profiles are in
[`case_evidence_requirements.csv`](../data/common_model/ecological_assessment_20260920/case_evidence_requirements.csv).

My interpretation is that the small R_H, R_C and R_J examples impose a smaller
**nondetection burden**, because they do not require a large undetected
population. Their demographic feasibility requires a separate check, below.
Several larger
examples already have mean refuge counts above 100 in 1995 before any lake
fish are added. If the published rough estimates concern the same population
and stages, those examples are in greater tension with the historical account.
That is a conditional judgement about examples, not a rejection of every
history at those parameters. It also crosses all four restrictions; it is not
evidence for a mechanism winner.

The two Bonnet Bay creeks cannot be converted into a fish-number ceiling.
Deep pools and cover can make fish difficult to find, and stream length alone
does not determine capacity. Conversely, invoking unseen fish requires a
credible location and survey-coverage explanation. The model has one aggregate
refuge compartment, not a spatial map of two streams, 117 survey locations,
or unsampled habitat. Its K_R is not a measured stream capacity or a hard cap
on realised abundance.

Barry's judgement that 50–100 fish could have persisted under inconsistent
spatial and temporal pressure remains relevant. It neither supplies a maximum
nor establishes that 100–200 fish were hidden in particular reaches. It also
does not identify a q applicable equally to a lake survey and stream fishing.

## Quantifying the nondetection burden without fitting q

For an archived history let E be the sum of natural-range counts in actual
states 1997–2001, the five states used by the frozen 1998–2002 observation
proxies. Under the existing homogeneous law the probability of all five misses
is (1-q)^E. Under the existing shared-quality law it is
0.5[(1-0.5q)^E + (1-1.5q)^E]. These calculations introduce no new observation
model or threshold. The 2002 terminal count does not enter this five-record
block.

For intuition, a constant hypothetical population gives:

| Count in each of five states | All misses, q=.005 shared quality | All misses, q=.02 shared quality |
|---|---:|---:|
| 20 | .625 | .207 |
| 50 | .344 | .0408 |
| 100 | .155 | .00329 |
| 200 | .0412 | .0000216 |

These are scenario calculations, not empirical probabilities. They show why
the field judgement about incomplete coverage matters: even 100–200 model
fish are not automatically incompatible under the low-q scenario. They also
show how much stronger the nondetection burden becomes at q=.02.

The already-selected surviving replicate examples give a similar contrast:

| Base example | Actual totals, 1997–2001 | All five misses, q=.02/shared quality | Same path, q=.005/shared quality |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| R_H minimum-survival role | 35, 3, 43, 5, 36 | .159 | .568 |
| R_C minimum-survival role | 2, 24, 1, 21, 4 | .399 | .777 |
| R_J minimum-survival role | 55, 3, 46, 1, 34 | .131 | .529 |
| R_H middle-survival role | 88, 225, 74, 228, 70 | .000512 | .0929 |
| R_T minimum-survival role | 143, 223, 34, 215, 38 | .000706 | .101 |

These are individual supported histories previously chosen by maximum factual
log weight within outcome, not typical trajectories or integrated source
likelihoods. Their joint compatibility also includes early patterns and positive
records. The late miss probabilities must not be multiplied into their weights
again. The contrast is descriptive reuse of fitted evidence, not a posterior
predictive check, Bayes factor or significance test. A low late-block probability
explains the burden on an example; it does not contradict its admission under
a permissive joint cutoff.

The alternating low and high annual counts in several examples also deserve
ecological attention. They require population dynamics that the available
capture summaries cannot test. We have not established that those fluctuations
occurred or that every compatible history fluctuates similarly. Inferring the
probability of a block from a mean abundance would hide that variation; the
calculations above instead use the actual paths. One specific low-abundance
feature can nevertheless be diagnosed directly.

## Single-fish rebounds expose a demographic approximation

In the historical kernel there is no external immigration or egg state.
Reproduction depends on the number of adults without sex or mate limitation.
Movement changes location, not the natural-range total. A whole-range state
of one fish followed by more than one must therefore generate recruits from
one modeled adult. A lone modeled juvenile cannot increase the total.

Such a transition is incompatible with a **literal complete, closed census of
individual fish required to reproduce within that annual transition, with no
unrepresented fertilised eggs, prior mating or immigration**. Hamr's breeding
observations document male and female fish and fertilisation. The recovery
plan's life-history account reports spring spawning, maturity around two years
and most breeding at three to four years. These observations do not support
interpreting the simulator as an explicit two-sex demographic model.

This is not an unconditional statement that a real survey recording one fish
cannot be followed by more fish. A survey can miss mates or eggs; its timing
may follow earlier reproduction; and model counts may not be literal census
counts. Those possibilities are precisely the mapping assumptions that need
to be made explicit. They are not represented as such in this kernel.

A bounded additional read of the archived histories checked the five
transitions between actual states 1997–2002. It retained the same 17 selected
source/profiles, factual weights and terminal-survival conditioning:

| Base minimum-survival example | Survivor-conditioned weight on at least one observed 1-to-more-than-1 transition | Monte Carlo SE |
|---|---:|---:|
| R_H | 1.37% | 1.09 percentage points |
| R_C | 19.76% | 3.63 percentage points |
| R_J | 23.03% | 4.17 percentage points |

All three survivor-weight sets meet the existing concentration criteria; that
does not remove endpoint uncertainty, particularly for the small R_H estimate.
The selected R_C example contains 1 to 21 between 1999 and 2000; the selected
R_J example contains 1 to 34 between 2000 and 2001. Six of the 25 individual
illustrations have a flagged transition (all six survive in 2002); four of
those six have unqualified outcome-conditioned weights. The high-q tails
are especially dependent on flagged paths but are too poorly resolved for
precise ecological conclusions.

This is a diagnostic of the sampled paths, not a newly estimated probability
of biological impossibility. It neither discards their weights nor says the
remaining paths are demographically adequate. Earlier years, mate availability
at counts above one, sex ratio, age structure and spawning success are not
resolved by this flag. Zero observed flags at other selected sources likewise
do not prove their absence under further simulation. Complete results are in
[`one_fish_rebound_diagnostic.csv`](../data/common_model/ecological_assessment_20260920/one_fish_rebound_diagnostic.csv).

The assessment therefore does not support simply preferring the smallest
surviving populations. Concealment becomes easier as abundance falls, while
the demographic approximation becomes more consequential. That trade-off is
part of the present equifinality, not independent confirmation of either end.

## What the two compartments do and do not mean

Inspection of the frozen common kernel
([recruitment and allocation](../src/galaxias_model/common_model.py),
`_advance_common_state`) shows recruitment proportional to A_L + A_R, with
habitat quality acting on the shared recruitment pool. PR006 allocates new
juveniles to the lake and 1-PR006 to the refuge. PR006 ranges from .561 to .855
in the selected sources. Explicit movement of surviving fish is lake-to-refuge;
recruit allocation is a separate coupling between the two compartments.

This can represent some combination of parental reproduction, early-life
dispersal and settlement, but the implementation does not identify their
locations separately. Even refuge adults can generate lake recruits through
the common allocation. Late lake counts consequently cannot establish a
demographically autonomous lake population, nor can refuge counts be assumed
to arise entirely from locally reproducing stream fish. The existing founder
viability checks do not resolve this spatial-demographic interpretation.

The historical contraction to stream refugia gives a late lake component an
additional explanatory burden: it needs a credible location, recruitment
pathway and reason for nondetection. At the same total abundance the frozen
late observation law does not distinguish lake-heavy from refuge-heavy
allocations. Unknown domain-specific coverage prevents turning the historical
distribution description into a quantitative ranking of those allocations.

Hamr's habitat and spawning observations provide reasons to scrutinise the
allocation, not a basis for setting PR006 to zero or changing the kernel now.
The low-gradient covered reaches are a defensible candidate refuge location;
unmapped steep upper streams cannot simply be assumed equivalent. Small
effective q may partly stand in for inaccessible habitat, but it does not
explicitly represent permanent blind spots, age-dependent catchability or
different effort between lake and refuge domains.

## Disposition and the question for further critique

**Retain the frozen comparison; qualify the ecological claims.** This assessment
does not add a hard exclusion, revise weights, or rank the four restrictions.
It supports the following distinctions:

- Extinction following the final detections and modest undetected persistence
  both remain viable narrative classes; neither identifies its causal mechanism.
- High-abundance persistence is a more demanding explanation of this record
  if the model's count scale matches the field accounts. Keep those examples
  visible as tests of the observation and population-scale assumptions.
- Low-abundance persistence is not automatically preferable: single-fish
  rebounds show that the sex-averaged demographic approximation can contribute
  materially to some surviving subsets.
- A lake component can persist through pooled recruitment and settlement;
  it is not evidence by itself of a second independent refuge.
- Compatible witnesses establish possibility within the declared comparison,
  not ecological adequacy or equal credibility among alternative histories.

The highest-value next discussion with Fable is whether the paper can present
these limits as conditional bounds, or whether literal population-history
claims require a small revision of the low-abundance demographic and spatial
observation assumptions first. This is a substantive scientific choice, not a
reason to start another broad search. The priority data would be Hamr's original
trip/site tables and IFS late-survey logs, especially sampled reaches, timing,
methods, sizes/stages and catches; the derivation and scope of the rough
population estimates are a second lead. These records would help separate
low local abundance from incomplete coverage. No request to their custodians
has been sent.

If those records cannot be recovered, a future sensitivity design could bound
coverage, recruitment allocation or mate limitation transparently. That would be a new
scientific design to agree, not a retroactive way of eliminating inconvenient
witnesses. Replaying more histories with the same uncalibrated observation
mapping would not by itself resolve this assessment's main uncertainty.

The conservation implication at this stage is to keep observation quality and
site-specific habitat and recruitment evidence visible in any recommendation.
This assessment does not estimate the effectiveness of removals, habitat
restoration or invader control, and it cannot rank those interventions.

## Verification and boundary

The small [analysis script](../scripts/assess_common_ecological_requirements.py)
checks the hashes of the five consumed interrogation tables, retains all 17
selected profiles and 25 individual examples, and verifies the five-record
probability identity. The additional raw-history input is checked against its
manifest, the selected original weighted survival values are reproduced, and
zero abundance remains absorbing throughout the observed transition window.
Its outputs and receipt are in
[`ecological_assessment_20260920`](../data/common_model/ecological_assessment_20260920/).
The earlier Monte Carlo qualifications remain attached; especially, both high-q
survivor tails are poorly resolved and are not used to define abundance bounds.
A bounded independent scientific check is recorded separately in this directory.
It confirmed the ecological qualifications and independently recomputed both
central R_C/R_J rebound masses and Monte Carlo SEs directly from the raw archive,
without importing the new assessment script. It also reproduced the six flagged
individual examples. No discrepancies were found; its corrections about the
count interpretation and low-abundance demographic demands are incorporated.
No simulation, reselection, likelihood change, commit, push, publication or
message to Fable was undertaken.
