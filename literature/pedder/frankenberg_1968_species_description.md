# Two New Species of Galaxiid Fishes from the Lake Pedder Region of Southern Tasmania

Roger Frankenberg

*Australian Zoologist* 14(3):268-274 (1968)

[Cleaned from a scan-based extraction. Tables and plate pages were omitted because the body text is the most useful component for LLM ingestion.]

## Introduction

Collections of galaxiid fishes from the Lake Pedder region of southern Tasmania by A. Neboiss and C. McCubbin in 1965, by W. D. Williams and party in 1966, and by the author in 1967, provided the material for this paper. Two new species are described, with remarks on their relationships and ecology. The occurrence of a third species in the region is also noted.

These collections represented the only records of galaxiids from a comparatively large portion of Tasmania between, and including, most of the drainage basins of the Gordon and Huon rivers. As galaxiids are the dominant element in the native fish fauna of the rest of Tasmania, it is not surprising that this little-known southern part should yield new species.

Aquatic habitats in the Lake Pedder region were divided on a physical basis into four broad types: Lake Pedder itself; swamps and slow-flowing streams; steeper streams draining the surrounding ranges; and small glacial lakes in the Frankland Ranges, such as Lake Surprise. At the east end of the Frankland Ranges, the divide between the Serpentine and Huon rivers lies in the plain and is scarcely distinguishable, suggesting that continuity of headwaters may occur during periods of heavy rain and thus allow movement of aquatic organisms between the two river systems.

The methods of counts and measurements followed Hubbs and Lagler (1958), except that the caudal peduncle was measured along its dorsal length and vertebral counts excluded the ural vertebrae. Abbreviations used in the paper are `LT` for total length, `LS` for standard length, and `f` for frequency.

## Systematic Account

## Galaxias pedderensis sp. nov.

### Type material

Holotype: a female, 49 mm standard length, reg. no. D.941, Tasmanian Museum, Hobart.

Type locality: Lake Pedder, southern Tasmania, near the mouth of the inflowing stream from Lake Maria, including the stream itself up to 100 yards from the mouth, 1 February 1967.

Paratypes: 19 specimens, 16 from the same locality and time as the holotype, and 3 from a pond about 400 yards south of the type locality, 3 February 1967.

### Diagnosis

Distinguished from all other species of *Galaxias* by a combination of a slender caudal peduncle, a single pyloric caecum, and a low number of vertebrae.

### Description

Description based on the type and 19 paratypes, 32.0-70.0 mm LS, of which 7 specimens were cleared and stained. The body is fusiform, with a flat dorsal head profile and a depressed interorbital region. Jaws are subequal anteriorly, and the posterior extent of the maxilla varies between verticals from the anterior one-fifth to one-half of the eye. Premaxilla and dentary each bear about 12 to 16 teeth, with additional teeth on the mesopterygoid and lingual plate.

One large open pore lies beside each tubular anterior nostril and another anteromedial to each posterior nostril. Additional pores occur in the interorbital, orbital, maxillary, mandibular, and preopercular regions. Gill rakers on the lower part of the first arch are two to three times longer than wide, except for the anteriormost one or two.

There is one short pyloric caecum. Least depth of the caudal peduncle varies between 0.32 and 0.37 of its dorsal length. Pectoral fins are rounded; pelvic fins bear a small unpaired splint ray; the anal fin begins slightly behind the dorsal origin; and the caudal fin is emarginate. Branchiostegal counts for 12 specimens ranged from 7-7 to 9-9. Vertebral counts for 17 specimens ranged from 49 to 52 in total.

In alcohol, the upper body and sides are irregularly blotched from dark brown to light grey, with a lighter ventral surface. In life, the upper body and sides are light yellow-brown with blackish-brown blotches, the lower sides are grey-green, and the ventral surface is whitish. Fins are light yellow-brown at the base and colourless distally.

### Size, breeding, and habitat

Maximum observed size was 80 mm LT and 70 mm LS, with females larger than males. In 10 specimens examined for breeding condition, only the 6 largest showed immature gonad development. The species was abundant near the edge of Lake Pedder and in a flowing stream entering the lake, with a few specimens also collected from a nearby pond.

### Affinities and distribution

*G. pedderensis* appears most closely related to *G. johnstoni* Scott, but differs in having fewer vertebrae, a single pyloric caecum, a narrower caudal peduncle, and a relatively longer head. It was known only from Lake Pedder and its immediate surroundings in southern Tasmania.

## Galaxias parvus sp. nov.

### Type material

Holotype: a female, 41 mm standard length, reg. no. D.940, Tasmanian Museum, Hobart. The name *parvus* is from the Latin meaning "small."

Type locality: pond on the east bank of Lake Pedder, about 400 yards south of the stream entering Lake Pedder from Lake Maria, southern Tasmania, 1 and 3 February 1967.

Paratypes: 19 specimens, 17 collected at the same time and locality as the holotype, and 2 from Lake Pedder near the mouth of an inflowing stream about 400 yards north of the type locality, 1 February 1967.

### Diagnosis

Distinguished from all other species of *Galaxias* by a combination of a rounded caudal fin and low numbers of vertebrae and pelvic-fin rays.

### Description

Description based mainly on the holotype and 9 paratypes, 22.5-54.0 mm LS, with osteological features and branchiostegal counts derived from 7 cleared and stained paratypes. The body is fusiform, the head is flat between the orbits with a rounded anterior profile, and the jaws are equal anteriorly or with the lower jaw slightly projecting. Dentition is well developed on the premaxilla, dentary, lingual plate, and mesopterygoid.

The sensory pore pattern is similar in general arrangement to that of *G. pedderensis*, though differing in detail. Gill rakers vary from about one to three times their basal width. Pyloric caeca are absent. Least depth of the caudal peduncle varies between 0.42 and 0.50 of dorsal length. Pectoral fins are broadly rounded, pelvic fins have a splint ray, dorsal and anal fins are rounded, and the caudal fin is truncate to rounded.

Branchiostegal counts for 7 specimens ranged from 7-6 to 8-7. Vertebral counts for 15 specimens ranged from 44 to 47 in total. In alcohol, the body is light grey-brown to dark brown above the lateral line with irregular blotches, lighter below, and whitish ventrally. In life, the back and sides are yellow-brown, the lower sides grey-green, and the belly whitish, sometimes with salmon-pink to gold coloration anterior to the pelvic fins.

### Size, breeding, and habitat

Maximum observed size was 60.5 mm LT and 54.0 mm LS. In 10 specimens examined, immature gonad development was evident in all, including the smallest specimen. The species occupied swamps, still pools, and backwaters, and was usually collected by sweeping a dip net through marginal vegetation. Juveniles were also taken in shallow open water at the edge of Lake Pedder together with *G. pedderensis*.

### Affinities and distribution

In size, general appearance, and several structural characters, *G. parvus* departs from the more usual *Galaxias* condition toward the Australian species *Brachygalaxias pusillus*. Nonetheless, it shows greater osteological similarity to *G. pedderensis* and *G. johnstoni*, and is regarded as phyletically closest to *G. pedderensis*. It was distributed in Lake Pedder and the headwaters of the Huon and Serpentine rivers in southern Tasmania.

## Galaxias affinis Regan?

Attention is drawn to a third *Galaxias* species from the Lake Pedder region, represented by two specimens, 50 and 75 mm LS, from Lake Surprise. These differ from the other two species in having two pyloric caeca, a markedly higher vertebral count, and a colour pattern of dark spots and irregular vertical bars. They may represent juveniles of *G. affinis* Regan, but further work was considered necessary before that designation could be used confidently.

Material examined: Lake Surprise (Huon River drainage), Tasmania, 3 March 1966 (2), W. D. Williams.

## Galaxias johnstoni Scott

Comparative material of *G. johnstoni* from the Derwent River system comprised the type series from a tributary of the Nive River and three specimens from Clarence Lagoon collected by J. Wilson on 27 March 1962. This was the total recorded material for the species available to the author.

## Summary

Two new species of galaxiid fishes, *Galaxias pedderensis* and *G. parvus*, are described from the Lake Pedder region of southern Tasmania and their affinities discussed. The existence of a third species in the area, questionably referred to *G. affinis* Regan, is also noted.
