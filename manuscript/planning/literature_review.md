# Literature review for the Pedder galaxias paper

4 October 2026. Prepared to support the paper plan and Chris Burridge's review
before manuscript drafting. This is a broad, documented narrative review of
the relevant literature, with a reference register and DOI import list. It is
not an exhaustive database systematic review. Access ranged from retained full
texts and official reports to abstracts; the register records that distinction.

## What the literature implies for this paper

The strongest paper combines the historical reconstruction with a precise
account of its inferential limits. The recovery story, the likely threats and
the difficulty of detecting a small population are already established topics.
The contribution would be to connect particular observations, survey conditions
and unresolved source accounts to the conclusions they support, then report
why four specified modelling procedures did not qualify for further historical
inference. The literature supports this discipline; it does not supply a causal
answer that the Pedder observations and model assessments failed to establish.

The closest precedents are [Chilcott et al. (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF12257)
and [Horner et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10592-025-01731-0). Chilcott
already documents the recovery programme, delayed confirmation at Oberon and
management lessons. Horner addresses current genetic diversity, differentiation
and introgression in the insurance populations. Our introduction should state
these contributions before explaining the additional value of an explicit
event record and tests of inferential reliability. Horner appeared online on
28 November 2025 but belongs to volume 27 (2026); retain this distinction when
reconciling Zotero metadata.

## Historical evidence and natural history

Frankenberg's 1968 description, [Andrews (1976)](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF9760297)
and [McDowall and Frankenberg (1981)](https://doi.org/10.3853/j.0067-1975.33.1981.195)
provide the taxonomic and distributional foundation. Hamr's 1992 report is the
central detailed source for remnant-population surveys, life history and early
recovery actions. Sanger (2001), Lake (2001), official recovery plans and
Chilcott provide overlapping accounts with different purposes and temporal
coverage. They should be linked as sources of particular observations, not
counted as independent confirmations whenever a later account repeats an
earlier one. Full details and links for sources without verified DOIs are in
`sources_without_doi.csv`.

The surviving record describes habitat transformation during impoundment,
an initial increase in Pedder galaxias collections, subsequent decline and
later detections in a few tributaries. Lake's faunal study provides important
context for the post-inundation productivity pulse and the sweep-sampling
series. The digitised fish index is not a lake-wide census, and the retained
figure has unresolved timing and error-bar details. The initial increase
rules out immediate elimination at inundation; it does not rule out delayed
habitat effects. Sampled lake zeros do not establish continuous absence from
every potential nursery habitat.

Hamr records a mating pair and a freshly spawned female in Bonnet Creek 2 on
14 October 1991. This corrects a mistaken earlier project summary. Creek
spawning is observed; survival of that spawn, natal habitat of the adults and
two identified creek-born generations are not established. The correction is
documented in `docs/hamr_spawning_erratum_20261004.md` and does not alter the
completed numerical results. The last natural-range capture is reported as
6 March 1996, with unsuccessful annual searches subsequently reported through
2002. Keep that as a last detection and survey history, distinct from an
estimated date of death of the last individual.

The dates also permit a conditional recruitment inference. The last positive
sweep in Lake's retained Figure 6 series was in 1980; the index was zero on
the later sampled visits in 1983, 1986 and 1989. If six years is a valid upper lifespan bound,
fish caught from 1990 onwards were born after 1980, and a fish caught in 1996
was born around 1990 or later. Under that bound, the 1996 fish supports
recruitment approximately a decade after the last positive retained sweep.
Hamr's small, presumed one-year-old fish in 1989–90 and 1990–91 provide
additional, age-qualified evidence of recruitment. Neither argument locates
the nursery: fish could have developed in creeks, lake habitat missed by
surveys, or used both across life stages. Creek spawning followed by lake
nursery use is one possible sequence. Nor do the dates demonstrate continuous annual breeding.
Hamr's age estimates came from limited specimens, so the upper-lifespan
assumption and the possible life-history sequences need Chris's review.

Historical ecology provides a useful framework for this work. [Haidvogl et al.
(2014)](https://doi.org/10.1111/eff.12103) distinguish types of documentary
evidence and their biases; [Haidvogl et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00027-015-0400-0)
show how reconstructing riverine fish communities depends on interdisciplinary
source interpretation. [Boakes et al. (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1000385)
demonstrate how spatial and temporal observation biases distort biodiversity
records. These sources support an event table containing source identity,
observation date, locality, method, effort, outcome and uncertainty. They do
not justify converting qualitative historical statements into measured counts.

## Trout, other galaxiids and habitat change

There is substantial comparative evidence that introduced trout threaten
non-diadromous galaxiids. [Townsend and Crowl (1991)](https://doi.org/10.2307/3545242)
document fragmented native-fish distributions associated with trout;
[Ault and White (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1577/1548-8659%281994%29123%3C0939%3Aeohsat%3E2.3.co%3B2)
consider habitat structure alongside trout in Tasmanian streams. [McIntosh
et al. (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00288330.1994.9516602) show that trout size
matters, and [Woodford and McIntosh (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1890/08-1909.1)
identify recruitment depletion and source–sink structure. These studies make
trout effects biologically credible without estimating their contribution to
Pedder's historical decline.

The literature also supplies reasons to avoid treating all encounters as
equivalent. Glova and Sagar's [1991](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF9910423) and
[1993](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1600-0633.1993.tb00093.x) studies describe dietary
and spatial relationships permitting local coexistence. [Jones and Closs
(2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/fwb.12637) emphasise life-history differences in
vulnerability. Regional syntheses by [Jackson et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00288330.2004.9517252),
[Hardie et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1002/aqc.722) and
[McDowall (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11160-006-9017-7) provide the broader
conservation context. The recent [Smith et al. (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1111/fwb.70280)
community study adds Australian evidence, but associations in seasonal South
Australian rivers cannot be treated as effect sizes for Lake Pedder.

Climbing galaxias requires separate treatment. [McDowall and Allibone
(1994)](https://doi.org/10.1080/03014223.1994.9517462) describe possible
competitive replacement following impoundment in New Zealand. That comparator
supports a hypothesis, not an established Pedder mechanism. [Jense et al.
(2025)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ddi.70072) show that surviving Clarence galaxias
genetic structure need not reflect a simple post-trout fragmentation history.
Use this as a caution about historical interpretation, not as a formal
negative control or evidence against trout effects at Pedder.

Habitat transformation and introduced fishes can act together. Their joint
presence, however, does not demonstrate synergy. [Lange et al.
(2018)](https://doi.org/10.1111/faf.12305) show that freshwater-fish responses
to multiple stressors depend on taxonomy and life stage. [Orr et al.
(2024)](https://doi.org/10.1111/ele.14463) review experimental approaches and
the distinction between the statistical interaction measured and the ecological
interaction of interest. The manuscript should distinguish simultaneous
pressures, sequential effects and a tested interaction. Its four model
restrictions do not exhaust all possible processes.
The [2023 Tasmanian listing statement](https://nre.tas.gov.au/Documents/Galaxias%20pedderensis_LS_APPROVED%20BY%20GM_15062023.pdf)
also treats the cause as unresolved and the introduced fishes as implicated.

## Founder accounting and conservation introductions

The source accounts require visible reconciliation. Hamr's dated releases of
18 fish in November 1991 and 12 in February 1992 total 30. The official
1997/revised-1999 recovery plan reports 31 in 1991–1992, comprising 20 wild fish
and 11 captive juveniles. A 1997 agency newsletter reports 18 plus 13. Sanger
reports 42 over a longer 1991–1992 window. Chilcott reports 34 over 1991–1997,
including a later addition of three, but its wording about wild collection
does not align cleanly with its separate account of captive offspring.
Horner reports approximately 45, including seven captive descendants. The
later three survivors came from four fish collected in January 1996 and held
until release in May 1997. These distinctions resolve some apparent differences;
the original records are still needed to resolve others. Arithmetic resemblance
between totals is not evidence for a particular reconciliation.

For Strathgordon, the early 74 transfers belong within the 353-fish programme
reported for 2001–2007. Recruitment was subsequently detected after habitat
works, but this before-and-after sequence alone does not identify the effect
of the works. Collection, captivity, mortality and release must be recorded
as separate events, and repeated accounts of the same event must remain linked.

[Lintermans (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF12306) places the programme among
Australian freshwater-fish recovery actions. [George et al.
(2009)](https://doi.org/10.1577/1548-8446-34.11.529), [Weeks et al.
(2011)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1752-4571.2011.00192.x) and [Bunnell et al.
(2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gecco.2026.e04423) support explicit source
selection, recipient suitability, genetic objectives and monitoring.
[Moy et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1002/aqc.4023) demonstrate why aquatic
translocation outcomes depend on source and recipient context. These sources
help discuss the programme fairly, including uncertainty faced at the time.
The breeding insurance populations are its demonstrated achievement. Continued
biosecurity, water-level management and assessment of recipient-ecosystem
effects remain part of that established recovery account.

## Nondetection and the Oberon example

Oberon provides a strong illustration with important limits. A January 1992
visual search was unsuccessful. Chilcott describes unsuccessful backpack
electrofishing during 1993–1995 in an embayment covering less than 10% of the
lake. An adult was recorded in February 1996, followed by recruitment evidence
in 1997. The official recovery plan says visits occurred at least annually,
but does not supply comparable exposure for each visit. The 1997 newsletter
and Chilcott differ over the month of the recruitment observations; retain
that difference pending original records.

The sequence shows that unsuccessful searches preceded later confirmation of
establishment. It does not establish abundance on each visit, continuous
presence at every sampling location, or a common per-visit detection
probability. “Three independent failures to detect a known population” would
therefore overstate the record. The distinction matters because method,
coverage, season, fish size and abundance affect detection.

[MacKenzie et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658%282002%29083%5B2248%3AESORWD%5D2.0.CO%3B2)
provide the foundational occupancy framework; [MacKenzie and Royle
(2005)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2664.2005.01098.x) address repeat surveys
and effort allocation. [Royle and Nichols (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1890/0012-9658%282003%29084%5B0777%3AEAFRPA%5D2.0.CO%3B2)
link detection heterogeneity to abundance. These methods clarify what a
suitable design records. They do not make sparse, heterogeneous historical
visits interchangeable or recover effort that was never documented.

## Extinction inference and rescue decisions

[Solow (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.mbs.2005.02.001), [Boakes et al.
(2015)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12365) and [McCrea et al.
(2024)](https://doi.org/10.1017/ext.2024.18) review methods for inferring
extinction from sighting records. Their relevance is that conclusions depend
on how observations arise, including variation in sighting rate, reliability
and search conditions. Method availability does not establish suitability for
the Pedder record. [Rivadeneira et al. (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1890/08-0316.1)
and [Caley and Barry (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0095857)
use simulation or alternative process models to examine such limitations.
The latter's coauthor is Simon C. Barry, not Barry Brook.

[Brook, Buettel and Jarić (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecy.2787) provide a
resampling approach for uncertain sighting reliability. Reliability of a
reported sighting and the probability of detecting fish during a documented
search are different uncertainties. The method is relevant background, not a
way to supply the missing Pedder survey exposure. None of these citations
authorises a historical probability from the present stopped analysis.

[Brook et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scitotenv.2023.162878),
*Resolving when (and where) the Thylacine went extinct*, provides the more
recent companion example. It examines extinction timing and spatial
extirpation using uncertain historical observations and sensitivity analysis.
It belongs beside the 2019 reliability paper when discussing how last records
relate to population fate. This is a comparison in inferential framing, not a
known-extinction-date benchmark or a calibration of Pedder survey detection.
The publisher abstract and highlights were checked; detailed comparison of
the methods awaits the full text.

The decision literature helps frame rescue without hindsight. [Tenhumberg
et al. (2004)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1523-1739.2004.00246.x) consider linked
wild and captive populations; [McGowan et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/conl.12285)
describe the role of ex situ management in conservation planning. [Brown
et al. (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0266244) formalise the timing
problem for moving an entire remaining population. [Rout et al.
(2009)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1365-2664.2008.01586.x) consider an eradication
decision using sighting records; that is a related decision problem, not a
Pedder rescue model. Our paper can acknowledge the donor–recipient dilemma
while stating that it estimated neither the effect of removals nor an optimal
rescue schedule. Cumulative collections are not a denominator for removal risk.

## What the modelling literature contributes

[Caswell (2001)](https://books.google.com/books?id=CPsTAQAAIAAJ), *Matrix
Population Models: Construction, Analysis, and Interpretation*, second
edition, supplies the standard background for age- and stage-structured
demography. Use it for the model's demographic framework while documenting
the project's nonlinear recruitment, mating and observation assumptions
separately. This verification covered the bibliographic record and limited
contents, not specific equations or life-history parameters. The verified
2001 edition has ISBN **9780878930968**; no book DOI was verified, and the
similarly titled book-review DOIs must not be assigned to it.

[Beaumont et al. (2002)](https://doi.org/10.1093/genetics/162.4.2025),
[Wood (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09319), [Hartig et al.
(2011)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2011.01640.x) and [Cranmer et al.
(2020)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912789117) establish the wider context
for inference with stochastic simulators. [Robert et al.
(2011)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1102900108) show why summary-based model
choice requires particular care. [Grimm et al. (2005)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116681)
support confronting models with several patterns, but matching patterns is
not by itself proof that historical mechanisms can be distinguished.

[Kennedy and O'Hagan (2001)](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294) and
[Brynjarsdóttir and O'Hagan (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1088/0266-5611/30/11/114007)
address model discrepancy and parameter learning. [Auger-Méthé et al.
(2016)](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep26677) show that even simple ecological
state-space models can have serious estimation problems. These are reasons
to investigate recovery and uncertainty, not diagnoses proving intrinsic
non-identifiability in the Pedder system.

[Beven (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhydrol.2005.07.007) is the appropriate
foundational reference if the manuscript uses *equifinality*: multiple model
structures or parameter sets can remain acceptable against environmental
observations. The paper develops that argument in hydrological modelling.
It does not turn our failed qualification tests into evidence that Pedder's
historical mechanisms are ecologically equivalent or intrinsically
unidentifiable. Include it where the term is defined, not as an explanation
established by the present results.

[Rykiel (1996)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3800%2895%2900152-2),
[Morris et al. (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086), [Conn et al.
(2018)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ecm.1314),
[Cook, Gelman and Rubin (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1198/106186006X136976)
and the [Talts et al. preprint
(2018)](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1804.06788) support explicit objectives,
simulation-based checking and distinction between implementation correctness
and inferential performance. Cook's posterior-quantile checks and Talts's
simulation-based calibration concern Bayesian computation under a specified
model. Our synthetic recovery assessments share the principle of testing
against known simulated outcomes, but do not implement those posterior-rank
procedures or establish empirical ecological adequacy.
The [ODD protocol update](https://doi.org/10.18564/jasss.4259)
helps organise model description. [Owen's Monte Carlo text](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/)
and [Vehtari et al. (2024)](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume25/19-556.html)
explain weight concentration and Monte Carlo diagnostics. Citation does not
imply that every technique in these sources was implemented here.

[Gneiting and Raftery (2007)](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)
provide the rationale for proper scoring rules in probabilistic prediction,
including the log and Brier scores specified in the terminal-probability
protocol. Their framework helps distinguish calibration from useful
predictive concentration. It does not validate the project's chosen
precision tolerances or supply the unknown historical terminal state needed
to score a prediction against reality.

For the manuscript, preserve the four distinct outcomes: inadequate independent
loss-class representation; inadequate representation after lake conditioning;
insufficient computational precision or calibration certainty for terminal
probabilities; and unstable early-mechanism sets between split simulation
banks. The first two did not assess discrimination, the third did not decisively
reject calibration, and the fourth did not establish decisive coverage failure.
They prevented the intended historical inferences. Protocols preceded their
individual production runs, while later designs responded to earlier results;
describe this sequence accurately rather than claiming formal public
preregistration of the entire programme.

## Current genetics, refuges and monitoring

[Adams et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF22263) provide the Tasmanian
taxonomic context, while [Waters et al. (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpad147)
show why contemporary distributions need not reveal introgression timing.
Horner's insurance-population study reports similar heterozygosity and low
differentiation, with evidence of introgression whose timing remains uncertain.
The absence of pre-decline Pedder samples prevents direct measurement of
diversity lost during decline. Neither current diversity nor introgression
provides a demonstrated cause of the historical collapse.

Broader assessments by [Lintermans et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1071/PC19053)
and [Lintermans et al. (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biocon.2024.110843)
place the case within Australian freshwater-fish conservation. [Jolly et al.
(2024)](https://doi.org/10.1002/aqc.4220) and [Jolly et al.
(2025)](https://doi.org/10.1002/rra.4447) discuss protection and fragmentation
risks from barriers. [Allan et al. (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1071/MF24221)
provide tools for identifying candidate trout refuges, and [Coughlan et al.
(2025)](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.72480) consider trait-dependent risk.
Fish-free habitat still requires evaluation for suitability and receiving-
ecosystem effects; isolation alone does not establish a safe introduction site.

Current agency records should update the discussion separately from the model's
historical windows. The IFS 2023–2024 report records 135 Strathgordon fish in
12 overnight fyke nets in April 2024, with a timing caveat for absent
young-of-year detections. The [May 2026 IFS report](https://www.ifs.tas.gov.au/news/2026/may/20/conserving-the-threatened-pedder-galaxias)
describes 100 fish moved from Oberon to Strathgordon that April and the agency's
assessment that fish remained abundant at Oberon. These are management updates,
not new calibration data or measured effects of the augmentation.

eDNA is a plausible complement to conventional monitoring. [Goldberg et al.
(2016)](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12595), [Deiner et al.
(2017)](https://doi.org/10.1111/mec.14350) and [Liu et al.
(2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jenvman.2025.124531) cover validation,
sampling and interpretation. [Smart et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1890/14-1751.1)
compare detection for an invasive smooth newt, not a fish; their [2016
cost study](https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.12598) shows why efficiency
depends on assay and sampling costs. [Tingley et al.
(2021)](https://doi.org/10.1002/edn3.124) provide the closer threatened-fish
example, modelling false-positive and false-negative detections of
*Galaxiella pusilla*. The Lachlan report is additional Australian fish context.
None establishes a validated Pedder assay, a calibrated late historical
detection rate or a cheapest monitoring strategy for this species.

## Priorities for drafting and further reading

The core introduction and discussion should draw mainly on the direct Pedder
sources, the closest galaxiid comparisons, the detection literature and the
rescue decision papers. Keep most simulator methodology in the supplement.
The complete DOI list is a library acquisition list, not a requirement to cite
every item in the paper. Register priorities distinguish core sources from
supporting or optional context.

Before drafting, reconcile Barry's Zotero export with the register, confirm
final years for online/issue discrepancies, and obtain full texts for any
abstract-only source that will carry a detailed claim. Original Hamr tables,
late survey logs and transfer ledgers remain the most consequential historical
gaps. Their absence should be stated explicitly; drafting can proceed with
source-attributed uncertainty. Chris's comments may identify available records
or alter emphasis. Any invitation to Rob Freeman remains Barry's decision.

This review does not reopen the completed simulation programme. It supplies
the literature and evidential framing for the agreed MFR plan.
